Modern Cybersecurity Threats and how to defend yourself against them.

Browsing the internet in 2025 sounds like a nightmare. From annoying spam emails to websites that steal your data even if you don't click on them. How can anyone survive this?

Despite the dangers of the World Wide Web, billions of people use the internet and trillions of dollars are spent in e-commerce alone.

Getting rid of all internet viruses and vulnerabilities is impossible, but that doesn't mean that the internet is unusable. Here's a small list of online threats:

Malware:

Adware - A malicious software that shows undesired advertisements.

Malware - A term utilized for every malicious software.

Keylogger - A malicious software that logs everything you type.

Ransomware - A malicious software that encrypts your files and demands payment (usually in a cryptocurrency like Bitcoin or Monero) for the decryption code.

Spyware - A malicious software that steals the information on your screen and your data.

Virus - A malicious software that multiplies itself.

Cybersecurity/Online Terms:

2FA - Two-Factor Authentication, a system that requires two ways of verifying your identity before granting you access to a service.

Antivirus - Anti-Malware software that blocks malware.

Dark Web - The part of the World Wide Web that is hidden from regular internet users and the place where illegal activitity occurs, like the exchange of illegal and stolen information.

Deep Web - The part of the World Wide Web that is not easily accessible by regular internet users.

Firewall - A network security device that creates a barrier between a trusted network and an unsafe network.

Phishing - A fake email, text message or website that mimics another legitimate website in order to steal information and/or money.

Social engineering - A malicious user manipulates someone to gain access to sensitive information.

Spoofing - A malicious user disguise their identity as a trusted source.

Zero-day - A vulnerability that was only recently discovered and could or could not have been exploited by malicious users before.
